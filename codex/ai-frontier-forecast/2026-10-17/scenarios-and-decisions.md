## RSI acceleration trigger

Buddy's central hypothesis is that recursive self-improvement increases the rate of future capability progress. Track the Vals milestone date alongside independent evidence of closed-loop successor improvement and consistently measured capability-doubling intervals.

**Accelerate the scenario:** repeated autonomous research cycles produce accepted novel improvements, lab-cycle time falls, human bottlenecks shrink, and subsequent capability gains arrive faster on comparable measurements. Reassess forecast slope and product lead times together.

**Hold or delay the scenario:** proxy scores rise but end-to-end research throughput stalls, gains fail to reproduce, human judgment remains limiting, or compute, data, and verification absorb the expected acceleration. Record which bottleneck changed and revise that causal link.

Track algorithmic efficiency separately from spending and compute expansion. The April 2027 100x Human AI forecast remains its own target; the July–August 2027 RSI projection does not replace it.

## Three mechanisms to test

### Rapid platform shift

Strong autonomous workflows, manageable verification costs, and platform distribution reinforce one another. New entrants bundle insurance outcomes into agent workflows; incumbents lose customer-interface ownership before every core system is replaced.

Leading evidence: production agent transactions with auditability, reliable migration/integration, competitive total cost, and customers choosing platform-native distribution. Evidence against: impressive demos with persistent exception-handling costs or no repeat customer use.

Proposed response: make one constrained insurance outcome discoverable and executable by agents, with clear permissions and a verified result. **October 7 evidence update:** [Personal Agent Protocol](https://sierra.ai/blog/introducing-personal-agent-protocol) strengthens the case for testing discovery and delegated authority across platforms. Tentative design implication: keep the outcome interface portable and separate discovery, authentication, authority, execution, and receipts. Advance the scenario only after interoperable implementations and repeated customer completion; an announcement alone does not change its timing.

**October 11 update:** [Draft 0.1](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/spec), dated October 9, now meets the specification-publication milestone. Production interoperability and customer-outcome criteria above remain unresolved.

**October 13 update:** PAP's [current design partners](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/path-to-1) include OpenAI and insurer-related companies, while its [operations extension](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/extensions/operations) supplies a testable approval/retry contract. Proposed next comparison: evaluate one scoped insurance outcome against those semantics and [managed workflow orchestration](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/workflow-runs), including failure recovery, permission boundaries, human review and total cost. This strengthens the implementation pathway; it does not establish production distribution, change forecast dates or authorize an implementation commitment.

**October 16 implementation options:** [Mercury MCP](https://docs.mercury.com/docs/what-is-mercury-mcp) documents agent-created payment requests with approval enforced inside Mercury. Proposed insurance comparison: separate discovery, request preparation, authoritative approval and execution receipts. [Muse Spark Open](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open) adds a self-hosted/API deployment candidate; licensing, hardware footprint, data controls and workflow-specific quality need evaluation. Neither establishes insurance distribution. These remain research/design candidates, not delivery commitments.

### Uneven acceleration

Software construction becomes much faster, while data access, liability, procurement, migration, and organizational change constrain adoption. New architecture enters around the edges of existing systems.

Leading evidence: large build-time gains but modest full-cycle savings, hybrid deployments, and durable review bottlenecks. Evidence against: complete migrations and regulated workflows reproduce strong gains without exceptional support.

Proposed response: design modular bridges and instrument review effort; create a path from an initial workflow to deeper replacement.

### Capability or economics bottleneck

Progress continues but measured gains fail to translate reliably into the target insurance workflows. Inference cost, long-tail errors, security, or human oversight limits the business case.

Leading evidence: stalled comparable-task results, expensive retries, unresolved critical errors, or negative total cost. Evidence against: repeated independently evaluated production success at attractive unit economics.

Proposed response: retain reusable, auditable components and buy options through small experiments instead of committing to a broad migration.

Probabilities are intentionally unassigned until the scenarios have common horizons, market boundaries, and mutually exclusive resolution rules. They are useful causal alternatives today; assigning percentages now would suggest calibration that has not happened.

## Players and incentives

These are hypotheses to investigate, not assertions about every organization.

- **State insurance regulators:** consumer protection, solvency, accountability, and enforceability. Watch actual requirements and accepted audit mechanisms.

- **MGAs and underwriters:** speed, distribution, underwriting quality, and control. Watch binding authority, exception rates, and delegated workflow adoption.

- **Carriers:** loss performance, operating cost, growth, compliance, and continuity. Watch real build-versus-buy decisions and migration cost.

- **Software vendors:** recurring revenue, installed relationships, integration knowledge, and margins. Watch bundling, contract changes, platform partnerships, and agent-native offerings.

- **AI platforms:** adoption, inference revenue, ecosystem control, and distribution. Watch persistent agents, connectors, identity, payments, and portability.

- **Consumers and brokers:** trusted coverage, simplicity, price, claims outcomes, and assistance. Watch completed journeys and repeat use rather than novelty.

- **Infrastructure builders:** capacity utilization, power, compute supply, and margins. Watch cost, latency, capacity constraints, and regional availability.

- **Nations:** competitiveness, security, sovereignty, and strategic supply. Watch policy changes that affect model access or deployment.

## Proposed workflow experiments

### Measure the multiplier

Choose a representative task set and a declared comparison baseline. Version tasks and acceptance tests. Record accepted output, all human minutes, calendar time, failures, rework, compute expense, and critical defects. Repeat across people and tools to distinguish a workflow gain from an unusually successful demonstration.

Decision trigger: materially higher accepted output per human hour at equal quality and acceptable total cost supports scaling the tested workflow. Higher output with unacceptable critical defects does not.

### Find the preprocessed boundary

Compare a fixed component, a generated component, and a hybrid on the same workflow. Include validation, latency, maintenance, auditability, and exception handling. **October 7 test candidate:** compare [OpenAI Decisions](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions) with the existing approach for one low-risk routing or completeness check using synthetic or approved de-identified examples. Measure calibration, false positives and negatives, refusals, human escalation, p95 end-to-end latency, and total cost per accepted outcome. Faster classification supports adoption only if quality and review costs hold. This remains a proposed experiment, not an implementation commitment.

Decision trigger: synthesize a component when end-to-end benefits persist after validation costs; precompute or reuse when stability and assurance dominate.

### Prove platform distribution

Demonstrate one customer outcome through a scoped agent-accessible service. Measure completion, permission failures, review burden, and customer value.

Decision trigger: expand when real users repeatedly complete the outcome and the cost-to-serve supports the business. A connector listing alone does not establish distribution.
